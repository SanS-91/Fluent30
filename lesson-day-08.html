Day 08 · Opinions

Hear what people
really think.

Opinions are rarely expressed as simple “yes” or “no”. Fluent speakers hedge, soften, partially agree, challenge politely and change position as the discussion develops. Today you train yourself to hear stance — not just words.

Step 2 · Stance Language

Decode the strength behind the words.

15 min
Agree
Exactly. / I completely agree.
Strong, direct alignment with the previous point.
Partly agree
I see your point, but…
Signals agreement with one part before introducing a limitation.
Hedge
I tend to think… / It seems to me…
Makes an opinion less absolute and leaves room for other views.
Soft disagreement
I'm not sure I'd go that far.
Polite disagreement without saying “you're wrong”.
Qualification
That said… / Having said that…
Adds a contrasting point that modifies the previous statement.
Balance
To be fair…
Introduces context that makes the other side more reasonable.
PhraseLikely stanceListening clue
I see your point, but… Partial agreement The real position often comes after “but”.
I'm not sure I'd go that far. Soft disagreement The speaker is pushing back politely.
To be fair… Balancing / concession The speaker is acknowledging another perspective.
That said… Qualification / contrast Expect a limitation or opposing consideration next.

Transcript — Ava & Marcus

Ava: I actually think a four-day office schedule could work pretty well for us, especially if we keep one flexible day.

Marcus: I see your point, but I'm not sure I'd go that far yet. It sounds good in theory, but we still have teams that depend on quick face-to-face decisions.

Ava: Sure, but that doesn't necessarily mean everyone has to be in every day. I tend to think we'd get better attendance if the office days felt more intentional.

Marcus: Maybe. To be fair, the current setup isn't exactly working perfectly either. People come in, sit on calls all day, and then wonder why they bothered commuting.

Ava: Exactly. That's kind of my point. If we're asking people to come in, there should be a reason.

Marcus: Yeah, I agree with that part. That said, I'd still want to test it with one or two teams first rather than changing the policy for everyone.

Ava: That's reasonable. I'm not saying we need to roll it out tomorrow. A pilot would probably tell us pretty quickly whether it actually improves collaboration.

Marcus: Right. If we treat it as an experiment rather than a permanent decision, I'd be much more comfortable with it.

“I see your point, but…” Do not stop at the agreement signal. The real stance becomes clearer after “but”.
“I'm not sure I'd go that far.” A polite way to say the previous claim is too strong.
“I tend to think…” A hedge: the speaker gives an opinion without presenting it as absolute fact.
“To be fair…” Signals a concession or attempt to balance the discussion.
“That said…” Introduces a contrast that limits or qualifies the previous agreement.
“I'd be much more comfortable…” Marcus is not fully convinced, but his stance becomes more positive under certain conditions.
