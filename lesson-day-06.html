Step 2 · Casual Speech Signals

Know what to ignore — and what not to.

15 min
Fillers
like / I mean / you know
Often buy thinking time. Don't assign too much meaning to them.
Self-correction
at seven — actually, make that seven-thirty
The later version normally replaces the first one.
Backchannels
yeah / right / exactly / mm-hm
Usually signal attention or agreement, not a new idea.
Vague language
stuff / things / somewhere / whatever
Infer the meaning from context instead of waiting for precise vocabulary.
False starts
I was gonna— actually, never mind
The speaker abandons the first structure and restarts.
Ellipsis
Want one? / Coming? / You good?
Casual speech often drops predictable words.
Signal Example Listening action
actually Actually, make that seven-thirty. Expect a correction or change.
I mean I mean, we don't have to stay long. Expect clarification, softening or reformulation.
anyway Anyway, what do you wanna do? The speaker is moving back to the main point.
basically Basically, we're waiting on them. Expect a simplified summary.

Transcript — Liam & Zoe

Liam: You heading out?

Zoe: Yeah, in a minute. I was gonna go straight home, but I'm kind of hungry now.

Liam: Same. I mean, I don't wanna do a whole dinner thing, but I could definitely eat something.

Zoe: Right. There's that place around the corner — the one with the little outdoor tables?

Liam: Oh yeah. Didn't we go there after Maya's leaving thing?

Zoe: Yeah, exactly. It was pretty good. Actually, wait, I think they stop serving food at eight.

Liam: What time is it now?

Zoe: Like seven-forty? Seven-forty-five, maybe.

Liam: Hmm. Bit tight. We could just grab a drink there and figure out food after.

Zoe: Yeah, that's probably easier. I don't really wanna rush.

Liam: Cool. Give me, like, five minutes. I just need to send this thing and shut everything down.

Zoe: No worries. I'll meet you by the elevators.

“You heading out?” Subject/auxiliary ellipsis: casual English drops predictable grammar.
“kind of hungry” “kind of” softens the statement and often reduces toward “kinda”.
“a whole dinner thing” Vague noun “thing” carries enough meaning because the context is clear.
“Actually, wait…” A correction is coming. Prior information may no longer be reliable.
“like seven-forty?” “like” signals approximation here, not comparison.
“figure out food after” Meaning is intentionally vague: decide later what/where to eat.
