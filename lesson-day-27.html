Step 2 · Meeting Signals

Hear when discussion becomes commitment.

10 min
Decision
“Let’s lock that.”
The issue is no longer merely under discussion.
Conditional decision
“We proceed provided…”
The decision stands only if a stated condition is met.
Owner
“Priya, can you own that?”
Identifies who is accountable for the next action.
Dependency
“That depends on billing reconciliation.”
One action or decision cannot complete independently.
Reopened issue
“Before we close this…”
A topic that looked settled is being challenged again.
Parking lot
“Let’s separate that from today’s go/no-go.”
The issue matters, but it is deliberately kept outside the current decision.

Transcript — Launch-readiness meeting

Maya · Product:Let me start with the product position. The core workflow is stable enough for production. The analytics module is not. We have three open defects there, one of which is still producing inconsistent data under load. I am comfortable launching the core product on November 18, but I do not want analytics included in the promise.

Jon · Sales:I can live with that for new prospects, but twelve enterprise clients already have November 18 in their planning calendar. If we move the whole launch, we create a credibility issue. At minimum I need a version of the product they can actually use on that date.

Leo · Operations:From support, the date itself is not the biggest problem. Scale is. Training finishes November 12, but that only gives us a few days to see where the knowledge gaps are. If we go to the full customer base immediately, we are effectively testing the support model at maximum exposure.

Priya · Finance:And there is a cost angle. The vendor has asked for another six hundred thousand dollars to keep the temporary integration team through December. Some extension may be justified, but I do not want us to approve the full amount simply because the original scope slipped.

Maya:That is related to analytics, not the core launch. If we separate analytics from phase one, I can freeze the core scope today and give everyone a clean defect list by Tuesday at three.

Jon:Okay, but I need clarity on what Sales is allowed to say. If analytics is delayed, I do not want twelve account teams improvising twelve different messages.

Leo:Agreed. Also, I would strongly prefer phase one to be the twelve anchor clients plus a small existing-customer cohort, not the entire base.

Jon:That works for me as long as November 18 remains real, not a pilot we later describe as a launch.

Priya:I am fine keeping the date if we define what has to be true before we widen it. Otherwise we are just postponing the go/no-go decision.

Maya:Then let’s make it explicit. November 18: core product only, twelve anchor clients plus the selected existing-customer cohort. Analytics stays out until the December 2 release.

Leo:For wider rollout, I want support tickets from phase one below eight percent of active users, and no severity-one issue open. If we are above that threshold, we hold.

Priya:I want one more condition: billing reconciliation has to be complete by November 21. We still have a mismatch between the contract configuration and the invoice logic on two plan types.

Jon:Would that block the twelve anchor clients on the eighteenth?

Priya:No. It blocks the wider rollout, not phase one. For the anchor clients we can manage the invoices manually if needed. I do not want manual work becoming the operating model at scale.

Maya:Good distinction. Let’s lock that. Phase one is not dependent on billing reconciliation; scale-up is.

Leo:One more thing before we close it. If training finishes on the twelfth, I need Product available on the thirteenth and fourteenth for support simulations. Otherwise the ticket threshold is not really a fair readiness test.

Maya:That is fine. I will assign two product specialists for both days.

Jon:And I will confirm the twelve anchor clients by Wednesday noon. I will also tell the teams not to position analytics as part of the November 18 package.

Priya:On the vendor extension, can we separate what is required for core stability from what is required only for analytics?

Maya:Yes. I will break that out with Engineering. We should not make the six-hundred-thousand-dollar decision in this meeting.

Priya:Fine. I will review the revised change order and cash impact by Friday. Until then, no approval beyond the current commitment.

Leo:So the wider rollout target is December 2?

Maya:Target, yes. Automatic, no. We widen on December 2 only if support tickets are below eight percent, there is no severity-one issue open, and billing reconciliation is complete.

Jon:That gives Sales enough certainty. I am good.

Priya:Same here, provided the vendor extension stays separate from the launch decision.

Maya:Great. Then November 18 is locked for phase one. December 2 is the conditional wider rollout. We have owners and thresholds. Let’s close there.

“I can live with that” Acceptable, even if it is not the speaker's preferred option.
“At minimum...” Defines the least acceptable outcome.
“Let’s make it explicit” Signals a move from discussion into a defined decision.
“That blocks X, not Y” Separates dependencies so one issue does not delay everything.
“Target, yes. Automatic, no.” The date is an intention, not an unconditional commitment.
“provided...” Agreement remains conditional on a stated constraint.
