Day 12 · Business Vocabulary

Stop learning words.
Learn business chunks.

Fluent workplace English is built from recurring combinations: meet the target, run over budget, close the gap, stay on track, raise a concern, work through the numbers. Today you train these as complete meaning units so they are easier to recognize at speed and easier to use when speaking.

Complete at least two full listens before revealing the transcript.

Transcript — Nina & Omar

Nina: Overall, revenue is still broadly on track for the quarter, but we're a little behind on the service line.

Omar: How far behind?

Nina: About four percent against plan. It's not huge, but if we leave it as it is, we'll fall short of target.

Omar: Okay. What about costs?

Nina: That's where I'd raise a concern. External support is running over budget, mainly because we extended the implementation by two weeks.

Omar: Right. So revenue's close, but costs are putting pressure on the margin.

Nina: Exactly. I think we need to work through the numbers before we revise the forecast.

Omar: Agreed. Is there anything we can do to close the revenue gap this month?

Nina: Possibly. Two clients have projects that could move forward earlier. If both confirm, we'd probably meet the target.

Omar: And if they don't?

Nina: Then I'd rather revise the forecast now than assume the upside. We can still stay on track for the full year, but we'd need to recover the shortfall in Q4.

Omar: Makes sense. Let's work through the numbers this afternoon and agree the revised forecast tomorrow morning.

“broadly on track” Generally progressing according to plan, even if not every detail is perfect.
“fall short of target” Finish below the required or expected level.
“running over budget” Costs are exceeding the amount planned.
“putting pressure on the margin” Higher costs are reducing profitability.
“assume the upside” Build a positive but uncertain outcome into the plan before it is confirmed.
“recover the shortfall” Make up for missing performance later.
