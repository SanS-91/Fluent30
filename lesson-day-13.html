Day 13 · Meetings at Natural Speed

Track the meeting.
Not every sentence.

In real meetings, people interrupt, soften disagreement, change direction and leave some ideas unfinished. Your job is not to remember every line. Your job is to keep a live map of: speaker → issue → position → decision → owner → deadline.

Step 2 · Meeting Language

Recognize the meeting mechanics.

15 min
Interrupt

Transcript — Weekly launch meeting

Anna: Okay, main question: are we still comfortable launching the campaign on Monday?

David: From a marketing side, yes. Creative is ready, media is booked, and if we move the date now we'll probably lose the placement.

Mei: Sorry, can I jump in there? I'm not sure we're ready from a data perspective. The new customer list still hasn't been fully verified.

David: I take your point, but we're talking about maybe ten percent of the list, right?

Mei: Closer to fifteen. And the issue isn't just volume — some of the records have missing consent fields.

Jon: Can we come back to the main question? Do we need to delay everything, or can we separate the clean data from the unverified records?

Anna: That's what I was thinking. Launch to the verified segment on Monday, then add the rest once Data clears it.

David: That works for me, as long as the first wave is still large enough to justify the media spend.

Mei: I should be able to confirm the final verified count by tomorrow noon.

Jon: Sorry — just to make sure I've got this right — we're not delaying the campaign, we're limiting the first release?

Anna: Correct. Monday stays. Unverified records stay out.

David: Okay. If Mei confirms the count by noon tomorrow, I can adjust the media split by end of day.

Anna: Great. Let's lock that in. Mei owns the verified count by noon; David updates the media split by five; and I'll send the final go/no-go note after that.

Jon: Works for me.

Mei: Same here.

“Can I jump in there?” Polite interruption. A new concern is entering before the group closes the topic.
“I take your point, but…” Partial agreement followed by a counterargument.
“Can we come back to the main question?” Meeting control language: the speaker refocuses the discussion.
“just to make sure I've got this right” Decision confirmation before action owners are assigned.
“Let's lock that in.” The decision is now final enough to assign follow-up.
“go/no-go note” A final communication stating whether the launch proceeds.
