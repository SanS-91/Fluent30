Transcript — Alex's voice message

Hey, quick update about tomorrow's presentation. I was going to send the revised slides last night, but I ended up changing the opening section again. The first version took too long to get to the point, so I've moved the market overview to the appendix and brought the recommendation forward.

I also cut one of the charts. The numbers are useful, but if we keep the data-heavy slide, I think we'll lose people before we get to the recommendation. I'd rather explain the key trend in one sentence and then walk everyone through the detail if they ask.

Could you take a quick look at slides four and five before ten tomorrow morning? I'm mainly worried that the transition feels a bit abrupt. If you think it works, I'll leave it. If not, just send me a note and I'll adjust it before the meeting.

One more thing: the client moved the call from two to two-thirty, which actually gives us a bit more time. And if you're free afterwards, a few of us are grabbing dinner near the office. No pressure, but let me know if that works for you.

What natural speech may sound like

“going to” → “gonna” I was going to send… often compresses heavily in casual speech.
“ended up changing” The consonants link across word boundaries, so the phrase may sound like one long unit.
“get to the point” Function words become weak; content words carry the stress: GET … POINT.
“if they ask” The final /f/ links directly into “they”; don't expect a clean pause between words.
“could you” → /kʊdʒə/ In fast US English, “could you” often blends with a /dʒ/ sound.
“let me know” A common chunk. Listen to the whole phrase rather than three separate words.
