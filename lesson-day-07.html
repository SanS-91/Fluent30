Transcript — Emma's airport story

So this happened to me a couple of months ago when I was flying to Singapore for a work event. At first, everything was completely normal. I left home early, traffic wasn't bad, and I honestly thought I had loads of time.

Then, about halfway to the airport, the car just stopped moving. There'd been an accident further up the road, and suddenly the whole expressway was backed up.

I checked the time and thought, okay, this isn't great, but I should still make it. A few minutes later, though, we were barely moving at all, and that's when I started getting nervous.

By the time we finally got off the expressway, I had less than forty minutes before check-in closed. I remember thinking, there's absolutely no way I'm making this flight.

Then all of a sudden, the driver asked if I was okay walking the last few hundred meters. I said yes, obviously, so he turned into this tiny side street, dropped me near a service entrance, and told me I could cut through the parking area.

I basically ran the rest of the way. I got to the counter completely out of breath, and the woman looked at me and said, “You made it with about three minutes to spare.”

In the end, I got on the flight, but I spent the first hour just sitting there thinking I am never leaving that little time again — even if I think I'm early.

“At first…” Signals the original situation before the problem begins.
“That's when…” Marks the moment a feeling or situation changes.
“By the time…” Connects two time points and often emphasizes delay or consequence.
“All of a sudden…” Introduces the main unexpected turning point.
“with three minutes to spare” Means three minutes remained before the deadline.
“In the end…” Signals the final result and often the lesson learned.
