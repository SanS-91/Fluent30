Day 15 · Negotiation

Negotiation English is rarely direct. People protect their position, soften disagreement, test conditions and exchange concessions. Today you train yourself to hear: position → concern → condition → concession → counter-offer → agreement.

Step 2 · Negotiation Language

Hear what the wording is doing.

15 min
Protect your position
I'm not sure we can go that far.
Soft disagreement without rejecting the discussion.
Trade one thing for another
If you can move on X, we can move on Y.
Conditional concession: your movement depends on theirs.
Signal flexibility
There may be some room on the fee.
Opens the door without committing to a number yet.
Ask for value in return
What could you do on payment terms?
Requests a concession before giving one.
Frame a compromise
That would make it easier for us to…
Links the other side's concession to your ability to move.
Close cautiously
I think we could live with that.
Signals acceptance without sounding overly enthusiastic.
PhraseNegotiation functionWhat to listen for next
I'm not sure we can go that far. Resistance Alternative proposal or limit.
There may be some room… Possible concession Condition attached to the movement.
If you can…, we can… Conditional trade Two variables being exchanged.
We could live with that. Qualified acceptance Final confirmation or remaining condition.

Transcript — Elena & Mark

Elena: Thanks for revising the proposal. The structure looks better, but I still have a concern about the fee.

Mark: Sure. Which part feels difficult?

Elena: At one hundred and twenty thousand, we're above the budget I can get approved. I need to be closer to one hundred.

Mark: I understand. I'm not sure we can go that far without changing the scope, though. The current fee assumes the full diagnostic plus implementation support.

Elena: What if we keep the diagnostic, but reduce the implementation support to four weeks instead of eight?

Mark: That helps. There may be some room on the fee if the support period comes down.

Elena: How much room?

Mark: I could probably get to one hundred and eight, but I'd need thirty-day payment terms rather than sixty.

Elena: Thirty is difficult for us. If we move to forty-five days, could you get closer to one hundred and five?

Mark: Let me think. If you can do forty-five days and confirm the reduced support scope this week, I think I can get approval for one hundred and six.

Elena: And the diagnostic stays exactly as proposed?

Mark: Yes. Full diagnostic, four weeks of implementation support, fee at one hundred and six, payment at forty-five days.

Elena: That would make it easier for us to get internal approval. I think we could live with that, subject to final legal review.

Mark: Works for me. I'll send a revised commercial page this afternoon.

“I'm not sure we can go that far.” Soft refusal. The speaker rejects the size of the requested movement, not the whole discussion.
“There may be some room…” Signals flexibility without revealing the final concession yet.
“If you can do…, could you…?” A counter-offer exchanging one variable for movement on another.
“subject to final legal review” The commercial deal is broadly agreed, but one formal condition remains.
“get internal approval” The deal still needs authorization inside the buyer's organization.
“revised commercial page” The pricing / payment / scope terms will be updated in writing.
