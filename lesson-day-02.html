Day 02 · Connected Speech

Stop listening
word by word.

Real English is compressed. Words link, vowels weaken, and familiar phrases can sound completely different from their written form. Today you train your ear to hear sound groups, not isolated dictionary words.

Complete at least two full listens before revealing the transcript.

Transcript — Chris's follow-up message

Hey, just wanted to follow up on the meeting. I should've said something at the time, but I kinda thought the discussion was going in circles. We spent so long on the background that we didn't really get to the decision until the last five minutes.

I don't wanna cut the context completely, but I think we've gotta make it easier to follow. I'm gonna move the recommendation up front, then give them two reasons and keep the detail in backup slides. If they wanna dig into the numbers, we can walk them through it.

Could you take a look when you've got a minute? I don't need a full review — just let me know if the flow makes sense. I'm gonna be tied up till around six, so if you send me your comments before then, I'll fix it tonight.

Written English → what your ears may hear

“should have” → “should've” The unstressed have reduces to /əv/. Listen for the whole chunk, not a separate word “have”.
“kind of” → “kinda” Very common conversational reduction. The phrase behaves like one sound unit.
“didn't really” → “didn' really” The final /t/ can disappear before another consonant, especially in fast speech.
“want to” → “wanna” Common in casual US English. Recognize the sound instantly rather than reconstructing two words.
“we've got to” → “we've gotta” The phrase compresses heavily. Stress falls on the meaningful words around it.
“going to” → “gonna” A high-frequency reduction. Train it as a listening chunk, even if you keep formal spelling in writing.
“if they want to” → “if they wanna” Function words weaken; the content word after them carries more perceptual weight.
“when you've got a minute” Listen to the phrase as one request chunk: not every word receives equal stress.
