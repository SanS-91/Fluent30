Test A transcript — Client workshop

Rachel: Have you got a minute to go over Friday's client workshop?

Ben: Yeah, sure. I thought we were pretty much set.

Rachel: We were, but the client asked if we could push the start from nine-thirty to ten-fifteen. Their finance lead has another call first.

Ben: Ten-fifteen? Okay. That actually might help us. I was a little worried the opening section was too rushed.

Rachel: Same. The other thing is they want two people to join remotely.

Ben: I see the logic, but I'm not completely convinced the hybrid setup will work well for the breakout exercise.

Rachel: That's fair. What if we keep the main discussion hybrid, then do the breakout as one full group instead?

Ben: Yeah, I'd be more comfortable with that. Fewer moving parts.

Rachel: Great. I'll update the agenda and send version C-four-two-one before six.

Ben: Perfect. And the room's still twelve-oh-eight, right?

Rachel: Yes, room twelve-oh-eight. No change there.

Test B transcript — Dinner reservation

So, last Saturday we were supposed to meet at this little restaurant downtown at seven-thirty. At first everything was fine, except one of my friends texted to say she'd be about fifteen minutes late.

Then, just as I was leaving, the restaurant called and said they'd accidentally put our reservation down for six-thirty. I thought they were saying we had to come immediately, which obviously wasn't going to happen.

Anyway, after a slightly confusing conversation, it turned out they could move us to eight-fifteen instead. That actually worked better because nobody had to rush.

We ended up meeting for a drink nearby first, and by the time we got to the restaurant, everyone was there and the table was ready. So the mistake was annoying for about five minutes, but in the end the later booking was probably better.

“pretty much set” Almost fully ready; only minor changes remain.
“I see the logic, but…” Partial agreement followed by a concern.
“fewer moving parts” Less complexity and fewer things that can go wrong.
“it turned out…” Introduces the real situation after earlier uncertainty.
